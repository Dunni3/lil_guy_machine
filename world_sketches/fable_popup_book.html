The pop-up book

  • three.js
  • paper engineering
  • crayon print
  • one room at a time
  • pages you buy

Round two found that one room standing with its neighbours flattened was the view that worked. A pop-up book is that idea with a physical reason behind it: the room you leave folds flat because the page is closing on it, and the next one stands up because its page is opening. The diamond floor of an isometric room is what a V-fold makes when the corner sits on the spine.

It also answers clay or crayon without choosing. The paper is the clay: flat card, real shadows, three tones from one light. The crayon is the printing on it. He stays round and soft, the one thing in the book that isn't paper.

What's real and what's faked

  • The walls are a true V-fold: two glue lines at 45° to the spine, crease along their cross product. They fold the way card would.
  • Furniture cheats. Each piece shears flat backward on its own page, timed off the same opening angle.
  • Everything printed is a canvas drawn once at load. The diary and the needs are canned, and nothing here talks to the sim.
  • He walks in straight lines through furniture. Not tried on the TV or in a headless browser.

How it could plug in

  • A spread is data: a floor print, two wall prints and furniture on room tiles, the same shape as house.js. Furniture only adds which page it sits on.
  • Snap-on rooms become page order. There's no floor plan to solve and no adjacency rules, and outdoors is more spreads in the same book. Where to go is a closed choice of tabs, which suits Jev and a D-pad.
  • Rooms he can't afford are already in the book as pencil pages. Buying one prints it. "Saving for the library" is a page he can turn to and look at.
  • The margins are the HUD. The day's diary line is the level-2 day memory in his handwriting, and the needs are crayon bars. On the TV there's no overlay left to design.
  • A day could be a page. At bedtime the leaf turns, and the left-hand stack is literally his past. Replay is flipping back.
  • His paintings hang in the print. The frames on the bedroom wall are slots for the pieces ArtStudio saves.
  • Night is a desk lamp. One light does the whole mood, so day and night cost nothing extra to author.

What I'd try next

  • Pull tabs and wheels as the verbs: a paper wheel that turns the window from sun to moon, a tab that slides him into bed.
  • Him choosing where a new piece of furniture goes, then watching it get glued in.
  • A town spread built from layered folds, so the outdoors gets depth without a heightfield.

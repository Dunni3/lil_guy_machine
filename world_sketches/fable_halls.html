Third pass by Fable. Two hundred years on, he is a man in a stone house at the edge of a dry plain. The house is drawn the way an architect draws one: cut through, in section, with him standing in it for scale.

The idea

Him. A man, at adult proportions, in a linen tunic, trousers and a dark mantle folded across the chest. No face, the way a figure in an architect's drawing has none. He walks, sits, lies down, paints, and stands with his hands behind his back.

The house. Five halls in a row, each eight metres high and nearly empty: plaster walls, veined marble underfoot and in the columns, a wooden table, a bench, clay jars, a rug. The light is the furniture. It comes through two slots, five slots, a hole in the roof and a colonnade, and it crosses the floor through the day.

The new things. One or two per hall, all in the same pale verdigris and none of them bright: a stone bed that doesn’t touch the floor, lamps that hang with nothing holding them, a page of light and a turning relief of the plain over the table, an orrery of hairlines over a plinth, a sundial whose gnomon floats, a line of light around each door. Outside, a ring across the sky, towers too tall to be towers, and something long and silent crossing.

One hall at a time. The frame is one bay of the section. Going elsewhere is a slow track sideways through the thickness of a wall, and he follows on foot through the door. The words are set in the cut stone above and below. The halls are big and he is small in them on purpose, so when he comes to rest the frame moves in on him, then gives the hall back.

The hall he doesn't own is unbuilt. Its stone is already cut and waits in the air above where it will sit, with the finished colonnade surveyed in line over it. Raising it sets every drum down.

What is faked

  • The clock, his thoughts, his state and the price are canned. Nothing talks to the sim.
  • Sun shafts are see-through prisms pushed from each opening along the sun, with dust drawn inside them. The lit patches on the floor are a real shadow map; the two agree closely, not exactly.
  • Bounced light is one point light sitting on the sun patch. Corners and ceilings are darkened with painted gradients.
  • He walks an aisle and a few fixed turns, so he steps through a bench now and then. The moon is the sun's lamp turned down and blue.
  • Performance is unmeasured off a desktop. One large shadow map covers all five halls and would be the first cost on the TV.

How it could plug in

  • A hall is data: a width, a roof kind, a list of openings, and furniture as boxes and turned profiles on a floor plan, the same shape of data as the earlier rounds.
  • The game clock is the sun. One number drives the light, sky, lamps and shafts, so quiet stretches that run fast would show as the light sweeping the floor.
  • Buying a room is raising it. Tiers that aren't granted yet are the stone in the air. One number per hall, zero to one.
  • Needs are a few words in the beam, top right. His thought is the line in the ground. Both are HTML over the canvas.
  • His art goes on the easel and, later, the walls: one plain hanging per hall would suit it. The page of light over the table is where his writing shows.
  • The shop could be the orrery's plinth: the same hairline model, turning, of the thing he is considering.
